III · The verdict

Daphne did it, in all three nations

Before the show, Daphne was a name for grandmothers: about 41 of every 100,000 girls born in England and Wales in 2018 and 2019. By 2024 it was 131, more than three times as common, and its rank rose from 382nd to 150th. The climb starts in 2021, the first full year after the premiere, and has not paused since.

Scotland confirms it. Its 2025 register, the newest in this file, shows 24 baby Daphnes against 6 the year before, a jump from 476th to 172nd. Eloise, the show's sharp-tongued younger sister, is the likely accomplice: up 40% in England and Wales and to 91st in Scotland. Kate and Anthony, the Season 2 couple, fell everywhere.

How the case was built. Counts come from the ONS (England and Wales, 1996 to 2024), NRS (Scotland, to 2025) and NISRA (Northern Ireland, to 2025), compiled for TidyTuesday by Nicola Rennie. Each name's count is divided by the total named babies of that sex in that year, so falling birth rates don't flatter or hide a trend. The multiplier compares 2024 with the 2018 and 2019 average.

Names with fewer than 3 babies are suppressed, and spellings count separately (Daphne and Daphnie are different names here). This shows timing that lines up with the show; it can't prove the show caused it.
